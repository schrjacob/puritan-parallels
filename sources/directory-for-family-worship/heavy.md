%% section: act
%% number: 
%% title: The Act

The General Assembly has carefully considered the matter and approves the following rules for encouraging genuine piety and preventing division within the church. Ministers and elders in every congregation are to make sure these rules are followed. Presbyteries and regional synods are to check that this is happening in their areas, and to correct or discipline anyone who fails to comply, depending on how serious the failure is. These rules will do no good if churches quietly let family worship slide, so ministers and elders must actively look into whether any families under their care are neglecting it. If they find a family that is, the head of that family should first be corrected privately. If the problem continues, the church leadership should give a serious, formal warning. If family worship is still being neglected after that, the person should be suspended from communion — barred from the Lord's Supper — as someone not fit to take part in it, until they change their ways.

%% section: intro
%% number: 
%% title: Concerning Secret and Private Worship

Public worship in our churches has, by God's mercy, been established here in great purity. But that alone isn't enough. Every believer also needs to worship God alone in private, and every family needs to worship together at home. As the nation is reformed, so should individuals and households be — real, lived godliness, not just outward profession.

%% section: sec-1
%% number: I
%% title: Secret Worship

First, about private worship: everyone needs regular time alone for prayer and meditation. The people who practice this most know its value best — it's how we stay in close fellowship with God and how we prepare properly for everything else. So it's not only the pastor's job to urge people, morning and evening, to keep this up. The head of every household is responsible for making sure both they and everyone in their care do this daily.

%% section: sec-2
%% number: II
%% title: Ordinary Family Duties

When a family gathers for worship, here's what that should normally include: prayer and praise — for the wider church and nation, and for the family's own situation and each person in it. Reading the Bible, with simple explanation so that everyone, even those who understand less, can follow along and get more out of it — both at home and later in church. Good conversation that builds everyone up in the faith. And when it's warranted, correction from whoever has authority in the family.

%% section: sec-3
%% number: III
%% title: Reading Scripture Together

Interpreting Scripture officially is a minister's job, not something anyone should take upon themselves without a proper call from God and the church. But that doesn't mean families can't read the Bible together — in fact, in any household where someone can read, Scripture should regularly be read aloud to the family. Afterward, it's good to talk about what was read. If the passage points out a sin, talk about how to guard against that sin in your own lives. If it describes God's judgment on sin, let that be a warning — the same thing could happen to you if you don't deal with that sin. And if it holds out a promise or calls you to some duty, use that to encourage each other to lean on Christ for the strength to obey and to take the comfort to heart. The head of the household should lead this, but anyone in the family should feel free to ask questions about anything they're unsure of.

%% section: sec-4
%% number: IV
%% title: Who Should Lead

It's the head of the household's job to make sure no one skips out on family worship, and normally the head of the household should be the one leading it. If someone is just being lazy about it, the minister should push them to do better; if someone genuinely struggles to lead, the minister should help train them up for it. Wealthier households are free to hire someone approved by the presbytery to lead worship for them. If the head of a household really can't do it, someone else who lives there — approved by the minister and church leadership — can take on that role instead, with the minister and session answerable to the presbytery for that arrangement. And if a minister happens to be staying with a family, he shouldn't single out part of the family for worship and leave the rest out — except in specific situations involving particular people, which, out of good judgment, are better kept private.

%% section: sec-5
%% number: V
%% title: Unqualified Leaders

Don't let random people with no real occupation — or wanderers just claiming to have one — lead worship in your home. That's exactly how people pushing bad theology or trying to cause division worm their way into households and mislead gullible, unstable people.

%% section: sec-6
%% number: VI
%% title: Keeping to One's Own Family

Family worship should stay within the family — don't invite or let in people from outside households, unless they happen to be staying with you, eating a meal with you, or there for some other legitimate reason.

%% section: sec-7
%% number: VII
%% title: Cross-Family Meetings

In hard times of persecution or corruption, cross-household gatherings for worship served a real purpose — things were tolerable then that wouldn't be otherwise. But now that God has given us peace and a pure gospel ministry, those same gatherings (outside the cases already mentioned) should be discouraged. They get in the way of each family worshiping on its own, undercut the public ministry, and over time fracture both individual congregations and the wider church. They also tend to cause real problems — hardening skeptics further and grieving genuine believers.

%% section: sec-8
%% number: VIII
%% title: The Lord's Day

On Sundays, both individually and as a family, everyone should first pray and ask God — who alone prepares our hearts — to ready them for worship and to bless the sermon and service to them. Then the head of the household should make sure everyone goes to church together with the rest of the congregation. Afterward, once the service is over, he should ask everyone what they took from it, and spend whatever time is left going over it together and talking about God's word — or, alternately, each person can spend time alone reading, thinking, and praying privately. Either way, the goal is the same: to make what you got out of church actually stick, and to keep growing toward eternal life.

%% section: sec-9
%% number: IX
%% title: Materials for Prayer

If you're able to pray in your own words, do that — it's a gift from God. If you're newer to this or less confident, it's fine to start with a written prayer, but don't just coast on that; keep working at developing an actual spirit of prayer, which God gives to all his children to some degree. Pray often and earnestly in private so that your heart learns to form, and your words learn to express, the right kinds of requests for your family. To help with that, here are some things to pray about and reflect on:

Admit to God that you're unworthy to come before him and unfit to worship him as he deserves — and ask him for the spirit of prayer.

Confess your own sins and the family's sins honestly — don't make excuses — until you actually feel the weight of them.

Pour your heart out to God, through Christ and by the Spirit, asking for forgiveness, for the grace to repent and believe, and for a life that's disciplined, honest, and God-honoring — and ask that you'd be able to serve him gladly, not out of obligation.

Thank God for all he's done — for his people generally and for your own life specifically — and above all for his love in Christ and for the gospel.

Pray specifically for whatever you actually need right now, spiritually and practically — whether that's health, a difficult season, or anything else going on in your life at that moment.

Pray for the wider church, for other reformed churches, and for your own congregation — and for anyone suffering for their faith. Pray for those in authority, for civic leaders, for your pastors, and for everyone in your congregation, including neighbors who are traveling and those at home.

Close the prayer by asking that God would be glorified as his kingdom comes and his will is done — and pray with real confidence that you're accepted by God, and that what you've asked for in line with his will, will happen.

%% section: sec-10
%% number: X
%% title: Sincerity and Diligence

Take this seriously, don't put it off, and don't let worldly business get in the way — and don't let skeptics or mockers discourage you either. Given how much God has blessed this nation, and how hard he's recently disciplined us, this matters. Leaders in the church and community should model this diligence in their own families, and also actively encourage other families under their influence to take family worship seriously too.

%% section: sec-11
%% number: XI
%% title: Extraordinary Duties

Beyond the regular routine above, families should also set aside special times for repentance or for thanksgiving whenever some unusual event — personal or public — calls for it.

%% section: sec-12
%% number: XII
%% title: Mutual Edification

Scripture tells us to look out for one another and spur each other on toward love and good works. That matters at all times, but especially now, when so much irreverence is around and people chasing their own desires think it's strange that others won't join in. So every believer should actively encourage themselves and each other — through teaching, encouragement, and, when needed, correction. Encourage each other to actually live out God's grace: turning away from sin and worldly desires, and living with integrity in everyday life. Comfort those who are struggling. Pray with and for each other. Do this especially when the moment calls for it — when someone's going through a hard time and needs counsel or comfort, or when someone has done wrong and needs to be gently corrected. If a private word doesn't work, bring in one or two others to help, following Christ's own guidance that a matter is confirmed by two or three witnesses.

%% section: sec-13
%% number: XIII
%% title: A Troubled Conscience

Not everyone has the gift of speaking well to someone who's struggling spiritually. So if you've tried the usual things — private and public — and you're still not finding relief, it's a good idea to go talk to your pastor or a mature Christian you trust. And if, because of your situation or for reasons of propriety, it would be wiser to have a trusted, discreet friend there with you for that conversation, then bring one along.

%% section: sec-14
%% number: XIV
%% title: Travelers

When people from different households happen to be traveling together for work or other necessary reasons, they should still walk with God if they want him with them. Don't skip prayer and giving thanks together — just have whoever seems best suited lead it. And watch your conversation: keep it constructive, not corrosive, so that it actually does the people listening some good.

%% section: closing
%% number: 
%% title: The Drift and Scope of These Directions

The whole point of these guidelines comes down to two things. First: to encourage real, active godliness among everyone in the church, in whatever role they have, and to push back against irreverence and mockery of religious practice. Second: to make sure that nothing calling itself "religious" is actually allowed to cause error, scandal, division, or disrespect toward the church and its ministers — or to become an excuse for neglecting your everyday responsibilities. Those things aren't the fruit of the Spirit; they're the works of the flesh, and they work against truth and peace.
