%% section: act
%% number: 
%% title: The Act

The General Assembly, after careful deliberation, approves the following Rules and Directions for cherishing piety and preventing division and schism, and appoints ministers and ruling elders in each congregation to take special care that these Directions be observed and followed; likewise, that presbyteries and provincial synods inquire and determine whether the said Directions are duly observed within their bounds, and reprove or censure — according to the nature of the offense — those found reprovable or censurable therein. And so that these Directions may not become ineffectual and unprofitable among some, through the usual neglect of the very substance of the duty of family worship, the Assembly further requires and appoints ministers and ruling elders to make diligent search and inquiry, in the congregations committed to their charge, as to whether there be among them any family or families who neglect this necessary duty. If such a family is found, the head of the family is first to be privately admonished to amend his fault; and if he continues in it, he is to be gravely and solemnly reproved by the session. After this reproof, if he is still found to neglect family worship, let him, for his obstinacy in this offense, be suspended and barred from the Lord's Supper, as justly deemed unworthy to partake in it, until he amends.

%% section: intro
%% number: 
%% title: Concerning Secret and Private Worship

Besides the public worship carried on in congregations, which has mercifully been established in this land in great purity, it is fitting and necessary that the secret worship of each person alone, and the private worship of families, be urged and established; so that, alongside national reformation, the profession and power of godliness — both personal and domestic — may be advanced.

%% section: sec-1
%% number: I
%% title: Secret Worship

And first, concerning secret worship: it is most necessary that everyone, alone and by themselves, devote time to prayer and meditation — the great benefit of which is best known to those most practiced in it. This is the means by which communion with God is especially maintained, and right preparation obtained for all other duties. Therefore it falls not only to pastors, within their own charges, to urge people of every sort to perform this duty morning and evening and on other occasions, but it is also incumbent on the head of every family to see that both they themselves, and all under their charge, are daily diligent in this.

%% section: sec-2
%% number: II
%% title: Ordinary Family Duties

The ordinary duties included in family devotion, when the family gathers for that purpose, are these: first, prayer and praise offered with particular reference both to the public condition of the church of God and this kingdom, and to the present situation of the family and each of its members. Next, the reading of Scripture, along with plain catechizing, so that those of weaker understanding may better profit from public worship and be made more able to understand Scripture when it is read; together with godly conversation tending to the edification of all members in the most holy faith; and also admonition and rebuke, for just reasons, from those who have authority in the family.

%% section: sec-3
%% number: III
%% title: Reading Scripture Together

Since the office of interpreting the holy Scriptures is part of the ministerial calling, which no one — however otherwise qualified — should take upon himself anywhere, except one duly called to it by God and his church; so in every family where anyone is able to read, the holy Scriptures should ordinarily be read to the family. It is commendable that afterward they confer together, making good use of what has been read and heard. For example: if some sin is reproved in the passage read, this may be used to make the whole family watchful against that same sin; or if some judgment is threatened or mentioned as having been inflicted in that portion of Scripture, this may be used to make the family fear lest the same or a worse judgment befall them, unless they beware of the sin that caused it; and finally, if some duty is required, or comfort held out in a promise, this may be used to stir them up to rely on Christ for strength to perform the commanded duty, and to apply the comfort offered. In all this the head of the family is to take the leading part, and any member of the family may raise a question or difficulty for resolution.

%% section: sec-4
%% number: IV
%% title: Who Should Lead

The head of the family is to take care that no member withdraws from any part of family worship. And since the ordinary conduct of all parts of family worship properly belongs to the head of the family, the minister is to stir up those who are lazy and train those who are weak, to fit them for these exercises. Persons of means remain free to engage someone approved by the presbytery to conduct family worship for them. And in other families, where the head of the family is unfit for this, another person who regularly resides in the family, approved by the minister and session, may be employed for this service, for which the minister and session are accountable to the presbytery. And if a minister happens, by God's providence, to be present in any family, he should at no time gather part of the family for worship while excluding the rest — except in particular cases concerning those specific individuals, which, in Christian prudence, need not or ought not be shared with others.

%% section: sec-5
%% number: V
%% title: Unqualified Leaders

Let no idler who has no particular calling, nor any vagrant person under the pretense of a calling, be permitted to lead worship in families or for them; since persons tainted with error, or aiming at division, may in this way be ready to creep into houses and lead captive foolish and unstable souls.

%% section: sec-6
%% number: VI
%% title: Keeping to One's Own Family

At family worship, special care is to be taken that each family keep to itself, neither requiring, inviting, nor admitting persons from other families, except those who are lodging with them, or at meals, or otherwise present with them on some lawful occasion.

%% section: sec-7
%% number: VII
%% title: Cross-Family Meetings

Whatever good effects and fruits such gatherings of persons from different families may have had in times of corruption or trouble — occasions in which many things are commendable that would not otherwise be tolerable — yet now that God has blessed us with peace and the purity of the gospel, such gatherings of persons from different families (except in cases mentioned in these Directions) are to be disapproved, as tending to hinder the religious exercise of each family on its own, to the detriment of the public ministry, to the splitting apart of the families within particular congregations, and, over time, of the whole church. Besides which, many offenses may arise from this, hardening the hearts of worldly people and grieving the godly.

%% section: sec-8
%% number: VIII
%% title: The Lord's Day

On the Lord's Day, after each member of the family individually, and the whole family together, have sought the Lord — in whose hands the preparation of hearts lies — to prepare them for public worship and to bless the public ordinances to them, the head of the family should see to it that all under his charge go to public worship, so that he and they may join with the rest of the congregation. And once public worship has ended, after prayer, he should ask what they have learned from it; and thereafter spend whatever time remains in catechizing and spiritual conversation on the word of God — or else, going apart individually, they should apply themselves to reading, meditation, and private prayer, so that they may confirm and increase their communion with God, so that the benefit gained from the public ordinances may be preserved and furthered, and they more built up unto eternal life.

%% section: sec-9
%% number: IX
%% title: Materials for Prayer

As many as are able to pray freely ought to make use of that gift from God; though those who are less skilled or weaker may begin with a set form of prayer — but so that they are not sluggish in stirring up within themselves, according to their daily needs, the spirit of prayer, which is given in some measure to all the children of God. To this end, they ought to be more fervent and frequent in private prayer to God, so that their hearts may be enabled to conceive, and their tongues to express, fitting desires to God on behalf of their family. Meanwhile, for their greater encouragement, let the following material for prayer be meditated on and made use of.

Let them confess to God how unworthy they are to come into his presence, and how unfit to worship his Majesty, and therefore earnestly ask God for the spirit of prayer.

They are to confess their own sins, and the sins of the family, accusing, judging, and condemning themselves for them, until they bring their souls to some measure of true humility.

They are to pour out their souls to God, in the name of Christ and by the Spirit, for the forgiveness of sins; for grace to repent, to believe, and to live soberly, righteously, and godly; and that they may serve God with joy and delight, walking before him.

They are to give thanks to God for his many mercies to his people and to themselves, and especially for his love shown in Christ, and for the light of the gospel.

They are to pray for such particular benefits, spiritual and temporal, as they stand in need of at the time — whether morning or evening — such as concerning health or sickness, prosperity or adversity.

They ought to pray for the church of Christ in general, for all the reformed churches, and for this church in particular, and for all who suffer for the name of Christ; for those in authority over them, for the civil magistrates, ministers, and the whole body of the congregation of which they are members, as well as for their neighbors absent on lawful business, and for those at home.

The prayer may be closed with an earnest desire that God would be glorified in the coming of his Son's kingdom, and in the doing of his will; and with the assurance that they themselves are accepted, and that what they have asked according to his will shall be done.

%% section: sec-10
%% number: X
%% title: Sincerity and Diligence

These exercises ought to be performed with great sincerity, without delay, laying aside all worldly business or hindrances, notwithstanding the mockery of atheists and irreligious people; in view of God's great mercies to this land, and of the severe corrections with which he has recently disciplined us. To this end, prominent persons (and all elders of the church) ought not only to stir up themselves and their families to diligence in this, but also to work effectively so that in all other families where they have influence or authority, these exercises are conscientiously carried out.

%% section: sec-11
%% number: XI
%% title: Extraordinary Duties

Besides the ordinary duties in families mentioned above, extraordinary duties — both of humiliation and thanksgiving — are to be carefully performed in families whenever the Lord, through extraordinary occasions (private or public), calls for them.

%% section: sec-12
%% number: XII
%% title: Mutual Edification

Since the word of God requires that we consider one another, to spur one another on to love and good works, therefore at all times — and especially in this time, in which irreligion abounds, and mockers, following their own lusts, find it strange that others will not run with them into the same excess of dissipation — every member of this church ought to stir up themselves and one another to the duties of mutual edification, by instruction, admonition, and rebuke; exhorting one another to show the grace of God by denying ungodliness and worldly lusts, and by living godly, soberly, and righteously in this present world; by comforting the fainthearted, and praying with and for one another. These duties are to be carried out on the particular occasions Divine Providence presents — namely, when someone seeks counsel or comfort under some calamity, trial, or great difficulty; or when someone who has done wrong is to be won back through private admonition, and, if that proves ineffective, by bringing in one or two others to join in the admonition, according to Christ's rule, that by the testimony of two or three witnesses every matter may be established.

%% section: sec-13
%% number: XIII
%% title: A Troubled Conscience

And because not everyone is able to speak a fitting word to a weary or troubled conscience, it is advisable that a person in that condition, finding no relief after using all the ordinary means, both private and public, should turn to their own pastor or some experienced Christian. But if the person troubled in conscience is of such a condition, or of such a sex, that discretion, modesty, or fear of scandal requires a godly, serious, and trustworthy friend to be present at that meeting, it is advisable that such a friend be present.

%% section: sec-14
%% number: XIV
%% title: Travelers

When people from different families are brought together by God's providence, being away from home on their particular occupations or any necessary business, they ought — if they would have the Lord their God with them wherever they go — to walk with God, and not neglect the duties of prayer and thanksgiving, but see to it that these are carried out by whoever the group judges best suited. And they should likewise take care that no corrupt talk comes out of their mouths, but only what is good and edifying, so that it may bring grace to those who hear it.

%% section: closing
%% number: 
%% title: The Drift and Scope of These Directions

The aim and purpose of all these Directions is simply this: on the one hand, that the power and practice of godliness, among all the ministers and members of this church, according to their various positions and callings, may be cherished and advanced, and all impiety and mockery of religious exercises suppressed; and on the other hand, that under the name and pretext of religious exercises, no meetings or practices be allowed that tend to breed error, scandal, division, contempt or disregard of the public ordinances and ministers, or neglect of the duties of one's particular calling, or other such evils, which are works not of the Spirit but of the flesh, and are contrary to truth and peace.
